#### a) what is a budget constraint (line) 
    Budget constraint line is a graphic representation of the tradeoff customers have to make in order to respect their budget limitations. Both axis represent the quantity of a certain product, such as X and Y, for example. The line always goes downward because of the budget limitations, and its slope represents the "tradeoff" coefficient. Basically means how many units of the product X the customer have to abdicate to buy another unit of the product Y.
#### b) what is an indifference curve ?
    The indifference curve is a graphical representation of the customer choice based only on his wants. Its curve is also goes downward, but it has a slope, caused by the power of trade off. It means that the slope changes during the curve, based on how much the customer is willing to make the trade. A good example is gasoline and kitkats. When you stop at the gas station during a trip, you have a limited amount of resources, which means you have a limit of gasoline and chocolates you can buy. Considering you're spending all your resources with gasoline, its quite likely that you could change trade one liter of gasoline to buy a chocolate. But if you already have bought one, it's less likely for you buy two chocolates, and it goes. There will be a point in the curve, where you already have too many chocolates, and you're not willing to not have enough fuel to get to your destination, just to have another chocolate. There is an optimal point for the customer, where he gets the ideal quantity of both products.
#### c) what does it mean when they intersect?
    When we put the the budget constraint and the indifference curve at the same graph, we can have a complete analysis of the two factors that affect demand: budget and preference. When we have an intersection between both line and curve, we have an optimal point, called the optimal bundle. This is the perfect spot for that specific customer.

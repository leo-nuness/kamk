# First Speaker - Shawn Kanungo
Shawn brings a reflection about the advance of AI, its use and how to take advantage of it. He considers AI the tool for “conventional wisdom”, meaning that with AI, we can do everything, but on average. The way AI is built, makes it limited to the already existing knowledge, being limited when we think of new inventions. He suggests people to be bold, try new things and explore unknown possibilities.
The speaker talks about AI evolution, and how the concepts on society are changing fast: in less than a decade, we moved from the belief that “knowledge is power” to giving value to execution, and now we are seeing AI agents that execute softwares and do tasks as we thought it wasn’t possible. For the future, judgement is power, and people that are able to judge and evaluate the AI projects, are going to dominate the market. He affirms that jobs will be extinct, but new ones will be created, similar to what happened during the digitalization of offices. With that, he suggests companies should rebuild their software infrastructure from ground zero, using AI, as an opportunity to create new possibilities.
At this point, I started questioning the lecturer and his affirmations, which sounded too optimistic and a bit sensationalist. He said AI is the most democratic technology, while he showed that he used really expensive models from the best companies, that only one prompt would cost a few dozen dollars. He brings important topics such as exploring AI, and getting familiar with the technology, because it’s a technology that will probably be part of everyday life from now on, but at the same time, he adds controversial sentences.


# Second Speaker - Rafah Harfoush
Rafah explained about the impacts of AI in our society, and the way we create connections with other people. Replika AI companion was cited as a big platform that offers the users AIs for relationships, and premium versions with erotic role play and other functions. During some adjustments to be compliant with EU privacy laws, some problems with the agents happened, and users had emotional issues. Rafah alerts that we can’t overlook these situations, even if they look weird or “dumb”, since it's going to become a more common problem. She suggests people try those relationship AIs, but with a researcher's look, with curiosity, safety and distance.
She shifts the lecture to focus on human behavior, and how this is the most important thing we have to differentiate ourselves from AI, meaning we should invest in expertise, to be able to evaluate the outcomes of AI, being an important part of the process. Also we should valuate the AI choices we make, because the way the model was trained creates ideology that affects all the company environment. This way, for every new technology, Rafah suggest us to reflect if the tool is aligned with the objectives and culture, and if the tool actually help.

Looking forward, for every new technology we create, she gives three questions, to think if the technology is prioritizing people:
- Are we investing in human expertise?
- Does it align with our beliefs and culture?
- Are we embracing curiosity?

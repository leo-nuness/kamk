# Module 3: Buyer Target Groups, Matchmaking and RDI

## Core Competence Analysis

### Topic 3.4: RDI Activities, Policy Frameworks, and the Kainuu Regional Action Plan (Group 4)

* **Core Question / Competence Addressed:**
  1. Why are Research, Development, and Innovation (RDI) activities vital to business transfers, and who are the key actors developing business transfers at the national (Finnish) and EU levels?
  2. What core empirical research results have been found regarding SME transfers, buyer hurdles, and support services?
  3. What policy recommendations have been proposed at the European and national levels?
  4. On a regional level (Kainuu), what concrete action plans have been created to support business transfers and renewal?

* **Theoretical Foundations & Literature Synthesis:**
  Research, Development, and Innovation (RDI) initiatives provide the empirical evidence base and policy tools needed to prevent viable SMEs from closing during generational shifts (Interreg Europe, 2020a; Kainuun Etu Oy, 2020; Van Teeffelen et al., 2016):
  * **Importance of RDI & Key EU/National Actors:**
    * *Why RDI Matters:* Across the EU, approximately 450,000 firms employing 2 million people are transferred annually, yet inefficient transfers cause the loss of 150,000 companies and 600,000 jobs every year (Kainuun Etu Oy, 2020). RDI activities link ownership change to business model renewal, product lifecycle innovation, and regional competitiveness, demonstrating that taking over an existing SME yields a higher survival rate and preserves more jobs than launching a start-up.
    * *EU-Level Actors & Projects:* The European Commission sets the policy agenda (1994 Recommendation, 2006 Communication, 2008 Small Business Act, Entrepreneurship 2020 Action Plan). Key European RDI consortia include *Transeo* (European Association for SME Transfer), the COSME *EU4BT* project, and Interreg Europe programs such as *STOB Regions* (Succession and Transfer of Business in Regions), *REBTES* (Developing Regional SME Business Transfer Ecosystems), and *SKALE2CT* (Skills and Scale to Connect for Business Acceleration Services) (Interreg Europe, 2020a, 2020b).
    * *Finnish National Actors:* Academic research is led by *Seinäjoki University of Applied Sciences (SeAMK)*. Policy and institutional actors include the *Ministry of Employment and the Economy (TEM)*, *ELY Centres*, the *Federation of Finnish Enterprises (Suomen Yrittäjät)*, *Finnvera Ltd* (state financier funding $\sim 50\%$ of all Finnish transfers, or $\sim 1,000$ deals and €100M annually), the *Finnish Family Firms Association*, *Finnish Enterprise Agencies*, and the *Finnish Business Transfer Society* (Kainuun Etu Oy, 2020; Viljamaa, Tall, Varamäki, Singer, & Durst, 2015).
  * **Empirical Research Results:**
    * *Demographic & Continuation Reality:* In Finland, $33\%$ of entrepreneurs are aged 55–74, and $28\%$ of all firms face a generational shift within 10 years (Viljamaa et al., 2015). National barometer data ($n = 2,830$) reveals that $38\%$ of retiring owners expect to sell to an external buyer, $28\%$ plan to wind down and close the firm, $20\%$ anticipate family succession, and $10\%$ expect co-owner continuation (Varamäki, Tall, Sorama, & Katajavirta, 2012).
    * *Buyer Bottlenecks:* Empirical buyer surveys ($n = 350$) identify business valuation ($3.0/5$), excessive seller asking prices ($2.9/5$), and financing ($2.6/5$) as the three most severe transaction obstacles (Varamäki, Tall, & Viljamaa, 2014).
    * *Hard vs. Soft Service Valuation:* Entrepreneurs rate concrete "hard-factor" services—Finnvera ($4.1/5$), commercial banks ($4.0/5$), accounting firms ($3.7/5$), auditors ($3.2/5$), and lawyers ($3.1/5$)—significantly higher than general public or municipal "soft" advisory services ($2.2$–$2.8/5$) (Varamäki et al., 2013).
  * **Policy Recommendations (EU & National):**
    * Shift from short-term, isolated projects to permanent regional business transfer ecosystems co-funded by government, municipalities, and business associations (Varamäki et al., 2012).
    * Give equal policy attention and financial instruments to buyers as to aging sellers (Van Teeffelen, 2012).
    * Transition from a reactive, problem-based model (focused solely on the owner's retirement age) to a proactive, objective-based model focused on pre-transfer business performance improvement, valuation, and product lifecycle renewal (Kainuun Etu Oy, 2020).
    * Provide innovation grants to digital matching platforms and advisory vouchers/subsidies so micro-enterprises can afford certified valuation and legal advisors (Van Teeffelen et al., 2016).
  * **Kainuu Regional Action Plan (STOB Regions PP5):**
    * *Regional Baseline:* In Kainuu, 800 out of 2,927 enterprises ($\sim 27.3\%$) face transfer or closure over a 10-year period, with an estimated 31 external transfers and 18 family transfers per year. SWOT analyses revealed that while hard-factor institutions (Finnvera, banks) exist locally, Kainuu lacked permanent, coordinated transfer services, suffered from youth out-migration, and had a high ratio of vulnerable micro-businesses (Kainuun Etu Oy, 2020).
    * *Imported Good Practice:* Kainuu adapted Southern Denmark's *Strategic Generation & Ownership Change* program (*Erhvervshus Fyn*), which links succession preparation with a 4-stage 360-degree business diagnosis and strategic growth/renewal plan.
    * *Action 1 – Business Transfers Services Platform:* Developed by *Kainuun Etu Oy* alongside the *Regional Council of Kainuu*, *ELY Centre*, *Finnvera*, and *Kainuun Yrittäjät* with a €300,000 budget under the national *Bridge Agreement (Silta-sopimus)* with TEM ($70\%$ public structural funding, $30\%$ local co-financing). It establishes: (1) a specialized *Business Transfer and Growth/Renewal Service Unit* (delivering 360-degree diagnostics, subcontracted valuations, pre-sale business renewal plans, matchmaking, and post-sale aftercare for 12 target SMEs); and (2) a *Regional Business Transfers Platform* coordinating municipal business advisors under a formal Memorandum of Understanding (MoU), upskilling staff, and registering 10 matches on national/EU platforms.

* **Critical Analysis & Real-World Application:**
  The Kainuu Action Plan illustrates a vital strategic pivot in regional economic development. Historically, public policy treated business succession as a demographic "aging problem," waiting until a founder was ready to retire before offering basic matchmaking advice. By that stage, however, the owner has often halted capital expenditures for years, leaving the firm with outdated equipment, stagnant sales, and uncodified tacit knowledge. Such a business cannot attract external buyers or pass bank credit committees. By importing the Danish *Erhvervshus Fyn* model, Kainuu reframes business transfer around the *product and business life cycle*. Conducting a 360-degree diagnostic and implementing a funded "growth and renewal plan" years before the sale upgrades the firm's operational cash flows and commercial attractiveness. Furthermore, uniting fragmented municipal advisors, ELY Centres, and Finnvera into a single coordinated platform eliminates service overlaps while subcontracting specialized tasks (like DCF valuation and legal deeds) to private experts.

* **Personal Learning Reflection:**
  Studying the RDI data and the Kainuu Action Plan fundamentally changes how I view the role of public business support and financial advising in rural regions. The empirical finding that entrepreneurs value "hard" financial, accounting, and tax services far above general counseling confirms that business transfers succeed or fail on financial fundamentals: bankable valuations, clean balance sheets, and structured funding. At the same time, Kainuu's shift from an age-based approach to a performance-and-renewal approach proves that valuation should not be used merely as a last-minute price tag. Instead, valuation and 360-degree diagnostics should be deployed early as strategic management tools to fix operational weaknesses, grow EBITDA, and ensure the firm is investment-ready for incoming buyers.

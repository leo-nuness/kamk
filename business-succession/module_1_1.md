## 1. Core Competence Analysis

### Core Questions Addressed:
1. What is awareness raising in business transfer, and why is it important?
2. Who are the beneficiaries of business transfer awareness raising?
3. What activities occur at different stages of the business transfer process?
4. What are good practices, models, and literature on awareness raising?

### Answers & Synthesis:

* **1. Definition & Importance:**  
  A business transfer means transferring more than 50% of a company's shares or assets to a new owner so the business keeps running (*European Commission, 2011; Viljamaa et al., 2015*). In the EU, about 450,000 SMEs transfer every year, affecting 2 million workers (*European Commission, 2011*). But one-third fail because owners plan too late, putting 600,000 jobs at risk (*Transeo, 2014; Viljamaa et al., 2015*). Awareness raising helps owners see selling as a normal business step and start planning 5 to 10 years early (*Van Teeffelen, 2012; Viljamaa et al., 2015*).

* **2. Beneficiaries:**  
  * **Sellers:** Get fair value for their hard work, save their legacy, protect employees, and retire smoothly (*European Commission, 2011; Krista et al., 2024*).
  * **Buyers:** Buy a working business with cash flow, customers, and staff, which is safer than a startup and easier for bank loans (*Orientations 2, 2024*).
  * **Employees & Society:** Transfers save an average of 5 jobs per firm (startups only create 2 jobs), and transferred businesses have a 90%–96% 5-year survival rate (*Van Teeffelen, 2012; Viljamaa et al., 2015*).

* **3. Activities Across Stages:**  
  * **PRE Phase:** Focuses on owner interest early using online checkups, emergency manuals, educational cartoons (using humor for emotional taboos), and roadshows for accountants and bank managers (*Tomas et al., 2024; Viljamaa et al., 2015*).
  * **TRANSFER Phase:** Technical help like valuation workshops, buyer-seller platforms, and tax advice (*Transeo, 2014*).
  * **POST Phase:** Mentoring, seller shadowing (3 to 12 months), and follow-up monitoring to stop failure after sale (*Ge & Campopiano, 2021*).

* **4. Good Practices & Models:**  
  The **BTAR Ideal Model** requires evidence-based and nationally coordinated work with two pillars: the **Business Transfer Barometer** (survey every 3 years) and a **National Advisory Board** (*Viljamaa et al., 2015*). Good European practices include **FÖRMER** in Austria (training young buyers), **Dutch 55+ direct mail** (toolkits sent to older owners), **TRANSF-BIZ** (Italy), **Succession Facilitators**, **Succession Checks**, and the **Kit Brunello System** (*Mohtisham et al., 2024; Transeo, 2014; Viljamaa et al., 2015*).

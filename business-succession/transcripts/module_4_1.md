## 2. Structured Summary

### Core Purpose & Scope

This presentation outlines the multi-tiered financing landscape required to fund SME business transfers, acquisitions, and generational successions. Because private buyers often lack liquid capital to acquire trading entities outright, facilitating transfers requires a mix of private equity, vendor financing, commercial bank debt, and risk-sharing guarantees from national agencies and EU institutions.

* **European Union Level:**
* **Guarantees & Counter-Guarantees:** EIB (European Investment Bank) and EIF (European Investment Fund) absorb risk for intermediary lenders. Programs like **COSME** (and EFSI/SME Initiative) lower collateral thresholds for commercial banks, targeting over 500,000 SMEs.


* **Funding Streams:** Direct funding (central EU calls for tender/proposals) and indirect funding (channeled via European Structural and Investment Funds [ESIF] through national managing authorities).




* **National Public Development Banks:**
* Specialized state-backed entities—such as **Finnvera** (Finland), **KfW** (Germany), and **Bpifrance/OSEO** (France)—de-risk transitions.


* **Finnvera's Toolkit:** Offers working capital loans, investment loans, growth loans for corporate restructuring, entrepreneur loans (allowing individuals to purchase equity in an existing Oy/Ltd), and counter-guarantees covering commercial loan defaults.




* **Regional & Commercial Intermediaries:**
* Commercial lenders (e.g., Aktia Bank, Osuuspankki/OP) provide term loans, asset-backed lending, and advisory services. Often, a commercial loan is a formal prerequisite to access state-backed bridge funding (e.g., Finnvera).





### Core Financial Instruments for Transfers

1. **Buyer Equity:**
* Direct cash contribution from the acquirer, personal loans (rarely sufficient for mid-sized valuations), or syndicating equity across a pool of co-investors.




2. **Subordinated Seller Loan (Vendor Note / Installment Sale):**
* The vendor finances a portion of the purchase price, repaid over time via fixed installments or percentages of operational turnover/free cash flow.


* Enables transactions where bank financing is constrained; aligns interests as the seller often provides transitional mentorship to protect debt serviceability.




3. **Senior Commercial Bank Debt:**
* Traditional debt secured by business cash flows, corporate shares, or asset-based collateral (machinery, real estate, equipment).


* Debt covenants and pricing reflect business profitability, customer concentration, and operational risk.




4. **First-Loss Capital & Debt Structuring:**
* Incorporating mezzanine debt, quasi-equity, and first-loss tranches (absorbing initial portfolio write-offs) to crowd in conservative institutional investors (pension funds, insurance companies).





### Special Case: Family & Intergenerational Transfers

* **Tax Optimization:** Family successions leverage specific statutory tax relief (e.g., Finnish inheritance and gift tax concessions for operating business assets), significantly reducing required external debt.


* **Advisory Demands:** Requires extensive professional guidance across legal structuring, fair valuation benchmarks, and non-commercial financing to preserve family solvency.
